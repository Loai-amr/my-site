01 flagship case study

The 2-second window

Most performance work gets reported as a score. This is reported as revenue, because that's what the business owner actually cares about — and the same discipline applies whether it's a WordPress lead-gen site or a Shopify storefront. Slow pages cost revenue before marketing ever gets a chance to work.

Client: Syslo Ventures — full-service marketing & production agency (sysloventures.com)
Scope: WordPress/Elementor performance audit & rebuild
visit site →
BEFORE
16.0s
AFTER
1.2s
Full page load, homepage — measured via PageSpeed Insights / GTmetrix, mobile throttled connection

What was actually fixed

  • Image pipeline — unoptimized, uncompressed uploads served at full resolution regardless of display size, converted to compressed WebP with responsive sizing
  • Render-blocking assets — CSS/JS loading synchronously in the head, deferred and minified so the page could paint before every script finished
  • Caching layer — no page or browser caching configured, added full-page cache plus CDN edge caching
  • Bloated plugin stack — redundant Elementor widgets and unused plugin scripts loading site-wide, trimmed to what pages actually use

Why 14.8 seconds is a revenue number, not a vanity metric

Google's own mobile UX research puts bounce probability at roughly +90% when load time goes from 1s to 5s. A widely cited industry rule of thumb: a large share of visitors abandon a page that hasn't shown them anything useful within the first couple of seconds. Applying an 80% drop-off at the 2-second mark — a number the business owner can sanity-check against their own analytics — to a site running at 16 seconds means the overwhelming majority of paid and organic traffic never saw the page at all.

// back-of-envelope, using the business's own numbers

$ assume 1,000 monthly visits to the page, 80% leave by second 2

→ at 16s load: ~800 of 1,000 visits bounce before the page is usable

→ at 1.2s load: page is usable before that 2s threshold — those 800 visits become reachable

$ if even a modest 3% of newly-reachable visitors convert to a lead

→ ~24 additional leads/month that were previously invisible to the funnel entirely

// this is illustrative math based on a common industry bounce assumption, not a guaranteed outcome — actual lift depends on traffic quality, offer, and conversion path. I'd always validate against the client's real analytics before quoting a number like this to a business owner.

The point isn't the specific number. It's that a marketing agency spending money to drive traffic to a page was losing most of that traffic to load time before any of their actual marketing — the ads, the content, the offer — ever got a chance to work. The same math applies directly to a Shopify checkout: every extra second before the page is usable is cart abandonment, not just a slow score.

Toolswift — B2B Admin Dashboard

React · TypeScript · Node.js

An internal admin tool used daily by a wholesale operations team, including a live area-selection map for defining delivery zones and shipping discount regions.

The problem: the map tool ran on Google's DrawingManager library, which Google deprecated. Losing it would have broken zone selection with no warning.
What I did: independently planned and executed a full migration to Terra Draw, rebuilding both the delivery-zone and shipping-discount selection tools, testing for accuracy against the old implementation, and shipping to a dev environment before rollout.
Business use case: for any company running location-based logic on top of Google Maps, this is the exact playbook for migrating off a deprecated dependency without downtime or a user-facing regression.
